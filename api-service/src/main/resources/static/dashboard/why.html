A snowboarder’s problem. A developer’s project.

Less time in traffic.
More time on slopes.

I snowboard at Keystone, and I’ve spent more than a few evenings stuck in traffic trying to get back to Fort Collins. I wanted to know whether leaving an hour earlier usually made much difference, so I started saving traffic readings.

Most of the drive out is on I-25 south and I-70 west. That’s where the tracker started.

The drive home shaped
the whole day.

We often had to choose between leaving early to get ahead of traffic and taking a few more runs knowing we’d probably sit on I-70 for hours. The mountains were getting busier, and snow made an already long drive harder to plan.

March 4, 2025 · Eastbound I-70

Left by 4:45 PM→Still stuck at 11 PM

Still on the way home.

Still short of the tunnel.

My two friends and I finished our second full day of riding and were on the road by 4:45 p.m. At 11 p.m. we were still stopped on I-70 short of the tunnel, watching wreckers squeeze past. When traffic finally moved, we crawled past stalled semis and stranded cars. Fort Collins was still a long way off.

That night made the news. Most of the backups we had to plan around were just regular weekend traffic.

What a live map

  • When did traffic first slow down?
  • Was it already slow by midafternoon on Sunday?
  • When did it start moving again?

About these days

Stored TomTom observations from this site’s archive-inclusive I-70 history, combining both directions. These September 2026 readings cover the then-tracked MM 206–259 corridor, before its October expansion. Each hour contains 59–60 observations. The three consecutive Wednesdays (September 2, 9 and 16) are compared with Sunday, September 20. Hand-picked examples, not a claim about every Wednesday or Sunday. All four charts use the same 40–70 mph scale, starting above zero.

Slowing and Clearing mean the average fell or rose by at least 1 mph from the previous hour. Otherwise, Moving means 66 mph or more, Slow means below 55 mph, and Steady is in between. Start marks the first hour in the example.

02 / From question to software

Writing the code was
only part of the work.

The tracker has to keep taking readings even when no one has the dashboard open. A page can load perfectly in the morning and still be missing hours of traffic from the night before. That’s why the monitoring checks when the last reading came in.

Running the site gave me a reason to try tools my coursework hadn’t covered. Docker let me run the API and the collector separately. I also learned to automate releases and keep the database separate so replacing a service doesn’t wipe out the history.
