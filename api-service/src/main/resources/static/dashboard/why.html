A snowboarder’s problem. A developer’s project.

Less time in traffic.
More time on slopes.

I snowboard at Keystone, and I’ve spent many long evenings in completely stopped traffic just trying to get back to Fort Collins. I started collecting traffic history to see which delays followed a pattern, rather than judge the drive from memory or a live map.

Most of my route follows I-25 south, then I-70 west. Those became the two corridors tracked by this project.

The drive home shaped
the whole day.

We often had to choose: leave early to avoid traffic, or stay longer and accept that we’d spend hours in it on the way home. As the mountains got busier, that tradeoff became a regular part of planning the day.

March 4, 2025 · Eastbound I-70

Left by 4:45 PM→Still stuck at 11 PM

Still on the way home.

One night that sticks with me.

My two friends and I had just finished our second full day of riding and left by 4:45 p.m. At 11 p.m. we were still stuck on I-70 while wreckers squeezed past. When traffic finally moved we crawled past semis and cars that were stalled or stuck. We hadn’t reached the tunnel yet and Fort Collins was still a long way off.

That was an extreme night, but the everyday delays were what I wanted to understand. Which stretches regularly backed up and when did they tend to clear?

01 / The missing history

I wanted to compare
one trip with another.

The maps I used were useful for checking the drive ahead in the moment. What I couldn’t easily do was compare the previous Sunday with a weekday and see how each afternoon unfolded. I started saving the readings so I could make that comparison after the traffic had cleared.

  • How does a Sunday afternoon compare with a weekday?
  • Does congestion begin around the same time each week?
  • How long does traffic take to recover?

About these days

Stored TomTom observations from this site’s archive-inclusive I-70 history, combining both directions. These September 2026 readings cover the then-tracked MM 206–259 corridor, before its October expansion. Each hour contains 59–60 observations. The three consecutive Wednesdays (September 2, 9 and 16) are compared with Sunday, September 20. Hand-picked examples, not a claim about every Wednesday or Sunday. All four charts use the same 40–70 mph scale, starting above zero.

Slowing and Clearing mean the average fell or rose by at least 1 mph from the previous hour. Otherwise, Moving means 66 mph or more, Slow means below 55 mph, and Steady is in between. Start marks the first hour in the example.

02 / From question to software

Writing the code was
only part of the work.

What started as a traffic question gave me a reason to explore tools my formal education hadn’t covered. I wanted to understand how they worked together in a project of my own.

Containers, automated deployments and monitoring each had a practical purpose. I needed to run the site, release changes and know whether traffic readings were still coming in.

I could try a tool, understand the problem it solved and decide whether it belonged in the system. Getting the site online was satisfying. Keeping it running has given me plenty more to learn.

03 / What I’m curious about next

Winter on I-70
Weekends and return trips

I want to see what
a full winter shows.

I want to revisit that Sunday-afternoon comparison once there’s a full winter behind it. Will the September contrast still show up during ski season? Will the same stretches back up each weekend, and how will those patterns change with the weather?

Some of what I expect may hold up and some may not. Either way, I’ll have a record to check rather than just a collection of memorable bad drives. I don’t expect I-70 to run out of material.
